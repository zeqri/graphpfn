# Graph-Level Pooling on a Frozen LimiX-16M Backbone

Summary of the experiment progression in this session. Distinct from
`dev/README.md` (an earlier session's work on multi-graph priors for
GraphPFN pretraining) — this covers a different line of work: whether a
small trainable pooling GNN can learn, in-context, to predict whole-molecule
labels using a frozen, already-pretrained LimiX-16M backbone.

## Motivating question

`limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_fit_test.py` (pre-existing) trains a `PoolingGNN`
against a **single, fixed synthetic dataset**, resampled once and reused for
every epoch. Fitting it well over many epochs doesn't prove much — it's
closer to full-batch gradient descent converging on one stationary target
than evidence the pooler generalizes across the prior the way the real
pretraining pipeline (`bin/graphpfn/pretrain.py`) actually operates: a
**brand-new synthetic dataset every micro-step**, with several micro-steps
gradient-accumulated into one optimizer update specifically to average out
the resulting variance.

The question this session explores: trained that way instead — multiple
fresh datasets per update, held-out evaluation on genuinely unseen draws —
does the pooler learn something that actually generalizes?

## Experiment progression

### 1. Multi-dataset training (`limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_multi_dataset_fit_test.py`)

Same frozen-backbone + trainable `PoolingGNN` (SAGEConv mean-aggregator)
setup, but matching the real pipeline's training loop: a fresh dataset per
micro-step, `N_GRADIENT_ACCUMULATION_STEPS=20` averaged per update, held-out
R2 tracked on fixed-seed datasets disjoint from training draws (a genuine
generalization metric, unlike the single-dataset script's same-split
loss/eval).

Result on a 300-molecule-per-dataset config: held-out R2 climbed through a
plateau around 0.28-0.30 (which looked, at the time, like convergence) and
then broke through to **R2≈0.52** by step ~800/1000 — a real lesson that an
extended flat stretch isn't proof of convergence while LR is still
annealing.

Repeating with the full 3000-molecule prior config (closer to the real
pipeline's actual scale) reached **R2≈0.56** by a similar step count and kept
climbing — the longer in-context sequence was both faster to learn from and
reached a higher ceiling than the shrunk 300-molecule version.

### 2. Widening the prior (`limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_multi_dataset_varied_prior_fit_test.py`)

Up to this point every sampled dataset used a fixed recipe: same graph
sampler shape, same SCM architecture, and critically the same `conv_type`
(`sage-mean`) and `graph_conv_ratio=1.0` for the label-generating SCM's own
internal GNN — meaning every dataset's label was generated by the *same*
aggregation mechanism, just with different random weights/topology.
`pretrain.toml`'s real prior varies far more per draw: `conv_type` (5
options: `gcn`, `sage-mean`, `sage-min`, `sage-max`, `gt`), graph size, and
much more.

This script widened two axes: molecule count per dataset (now
`log_uniform_int(--min-molecules, --max-molecules)` instead of one fixed
value) and `conv_type` (now sampled from all 5 options instead of fixed).
`avg_degree`, the graph sampler's own shape parameters, `causal.enabled`
(kept `False`, the setting under which atom features stay purely local/raw
while the label is still the full graph-convolved output — see "why some
conv_types are harder" below), and task type (this pathway only ever
produces continuous regression) were all left fixed by choice.

Result: R2 dropped substantially at matched step counts (down to ~0.30
where the fixed-recipe version was already at ~0.56) — expected, since the
task became genuinely harder and more representative, not a regression.

### 3. Diagnosing the plateau: per-`conv_type` breakdown

The training loop's own held-out eval only has 8 fixed datasets split by
chance across 5 `conv_type`s — nowhere near enough signal to tell "some
`conv_type`s are harder" from "a couple of the 8 fixed draws are unlucky."
Two tools were built to get a real answer:

- The training scripts' `evaluate_held_out()` was extended to print each
  held-out dataset's own `conv_type` (and later `n_molecules`) alongside its
  R2, not just the averaged number.
- A standalone diagnostic (`diagnose_conv_type_size_r2.py`) loads a trained
  checkpoint (no further training) and runs it against many held-out
  datasets **per `conv_type`, with `conv_type` forced** (not left to chance)
  and molecule count deliberately spread across a range — giving a real
  R2-vs-size trend per mechanism instead of 1-3 anecdotal points.

Finding: `gcn` and `sage-mean` fit very well (R2 up to 0.9+), `sage-min`/
`sage-max` capped much lower (R2 ≤ ~0.4), and `gt` (attention-based) sat at
essentially R2≈0 — **uniformly across molecule sizes**, ruling out the
initial "min/max becomes less informative on bigger graphs" hypothesis
(measured size correlation was near-zero or even positive, not negative).

### 4. Why: architecture mismatch

The base `PoolingGNN` had a single, fixed mean-aggregator (`SAGEConv`) at
both the per-layer message-passing step and the final atom→molecule
readout. Two reasons this predicts exactly the observed ranking:

- `mean`/`gcn` are smooth, parameter-free, universal linear aggregations —
  dense, stable gradients, exactly reproducible by a matching mean branch.
- `min`/`max` involve a hard, non-differentiable selection (gradient only
  reaches the single arg-min/arg-max neighbor each step) — much noisier to
  optimize through a shared branch.
- `gt`'s label-generating attention (`layers.py`'s `GTConv`) uses **freshly
  random QKV weights for every sampled dataset** — there is no single fixed
  "true attention function" a shared, trained module can converge to and
  reuse; each `gt`-labeled dataset needs its specific random operator
  inferred from context, which is the frozen backbone's job (in-context
  learning), not something a fixed pooler branch can precompute once.

### 5. First fix: `MultiAggregatorConv`/`MultiAggregatorPool`

(`limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_multi_dataset_multi_agg_pooler_fit_test.py`)
Gave every message-passing layer and the final readout access to
mean/min/max reductions *and* a lightweight attention branch, concatenated
and linearly projected back down — so the pooler at least has the raw
ingredients for all five `conv_type`s instead of only one.

Result: large improvement over the single-mean-aggregator baseline at
matched step counts (R2≈0.48-0.52 vs. the earlier plateau), confirming the
architecture mismatch was real and fixable to a first degree. But the
per-`conv_type` diagnostic re-run against this checkpoint showed the same
underlying split persisted: `gcn`/`sage-mean` strong, `sage-min`/`sage-max`
still capped low, `gt` still near zero — collapsing all four branches into
one combined embedding via a single shared, trained linear layer (`combine`)
before the frozen backbone ever saw the result was hypothesized as the
remaining bottleneck.

### 6. Second fix: `MultiViewPool`

(`limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_multi_dataset_multi_view_pooler_fit_test.py`)
Checked first whether this was architecturally sound: the frozen backbone's
`EncoderBaseLayer` (`vendor/limix/model/layer.py`) uses
`layer_arch='fmfmsm'` — real multi-head self-attention **across the
"groups" dimension**, not just across molecules — and `add_embeddings`
generates its positional embedding fresh from the runtime group count every
call (no fixed max-groups table), so exposing more groups than the model
saw at pretraining is safe.

Based on that, the final readout stopped collapsing mean/min/max/attention
pooling into one embedding, instead emitting all four as **separate groups**
(`n_groups → n_groups × 4`), letting the frozen backbone's own cross-group
attention pick which view matters per dataset via in-context inference,
rather than one shared linear layer deciding upfront, identically, for every
dataset regardless of `conv_type`.

Result, once training was stable (see note below on learning rate): **no
improvement** on the weak `conv_type`s. Re-running the per-`conv_type`
diagnostic against a comparable-overall-R2 checkpoint showed essentially the
same split as before — `gcn`/`sage-mean` still strong, `sage-min`/`sage-max`
still capped in the same range, `gt` still at ~0. Giving the pooler genuinely
more expressive machinery didn't move the needle on the conv_types it was
targeted at.

## Where this leaves the open question

The `MultiViewPool` result is informative precisely because it's a null
result: it argues *against* "pooler representational capacity was the
bottleneck" and *for* the complementary hypothesis raised earlier —
`sage-min`/`sage-max`/`gt` may be **inherently harder targets**, not just
harder to represent:

- `min`/`max`-generated labels are sharp, high-sensitivity functions (which
  single atom is the extreme one, and its specific value after 3 nonlinear
  layers, can flip on small perturbations) — much less forgiving to learn
  from limited in-context examples than a smooth linear aggregate like
  `mean`, independent of what model is doing the learning.
- This is compounded by `graph_conv_ratio` being held fixed at `1.0`
  throughout every experiment above — meaning *every* layer, including the
  one producing the label, goes through whichever `conv_type` got sampled,
  with no smoother linear-only channels mixed in (unlike `pretrain.toml`,
  which varies this too). That was a deliberate simplification early on to
  isolate the `conv_type` axis alone, but it may also be what makes the
  hard `conv_type`s' labels maximally sharp.
- `gt` specifically differs from the other four in kind, not degree: its
  label-generating attention weights are freshly random per dataset, so no
  fixed/shared learned operator — however expressive — can structurally
  replicate it; only the frozen backbone's in-context inference can, and
  it may not be well-calibrated for that given its own pretraining prior.

## Where things live

- **Training scripts** (`dev/limix_backbone_gnn_pooling_multi_dataset*_fit_test.py`):
  each is self-contained, CLI-configurable (`--min-molecules`,
  `--max-molecules`, `--n-steps`, `--lr`, `--n-sampler-workers`,
  `--prefetch-factor`), checkpoints/resumes automatically, and logs
  per-step metrics to `dev/output/<script-name>/<config>/training_log.jsonl`.
- **Diagnostics** (`dev/diagnose_conv_type_size_r2*.py`): standalone,
  read-only scripts that load a trained checkpoint and evaluate it against
  many held-out datasets per `conv_type` (forced, not left to chance),
  reporting R2 vs. molecule size per mechanism. Two variants exist, matching
  the two pooler architectures (`MultiAggregatorPool` vs. `MultiViewPool`) —
  they are not interchangeable, since the checkpoint's state_dict shapes
  differ.
- **Frozen backbone internals referenced**: `vendor/limix/model/layer.py`
  (`EncoderBaseLayer`, `layer_arch='fmfmsm'`, cross-group feature attention)
  and `vendor/limix/model/transformer.py` (`add_embeddings`'s
  runtime-shape-agnostic positional embedding) — both read directly to
  ground the `MultiViewPool` design, not guessed at.
